18th August 2026
WHO Divisional Officer Dr. Tariq Saleem Visits Chakwal to Review EPI and VPD Surveillance

Dr. Tariq Saleem, Divisional Officer at the World Health Organization (WHO), conducted an extensive official visit to District Chakwal to evaluate and strengthen local healthcare operations.

During his visit, Dr. Saleem carried out a detailed assessment of Vaccine-Preventable Diseases (VPD) Surveillance and the Expanded Programme on Immunization (EPI). He identified critical operational gaps and provided strategic guidance to healthcare staff on effective rectification measures to improve overall performance.

As part of his itinerary, Dr. Saleem inspected DHQ Hospital Chakwal, focusing on:
  • Ice-Lined Refrigerators (ILRs) to ensure strict cold chain maintenance.
  • Monitoring Charts to verify precise record-keeping.
  • QR Code & Electronic Medical Record (EMR) Integration to assess digital tracking efficiency.
Addressing the Lady Health Visitors (LHVs), Dr. Saleem emphasized the crucial role of complete data accuracy, instructing them to ensure 100% data entry compliance across both QR code systems and EMR portals without delay.

Sweep-up Activity for improving EPI coverage has started in District Chakwal. Phase-1 covers all Urban UCs across every tehsil; Phase-2 will cover the remaining UCs. Key Observations
  • Chakwal is relatively better on BCG→P1 and P1→P3; MR1→MR2 drop-out still high (+10.5%).
  • Kallar Kahar shows extreme P1→P3 anomaly (−220.8%) — likely data quality / reporting issue; needs urgent verification.
  • Choa Saidan Shah & Talagang have high drop-out across BCG→P1 and P1→P3 pathways.
  • Total UCs with high drop-out (>10%): 82 (Chakwal 36 + Talagang 29 + Kallar Kahar 9 + Choa 8).
  • Action: Investigate defaulters, session quality, and data anomalies in high-DO UCs.
Note: High drop-out is defined as >+10% or <−2% between consecutive doses. Negative values often indicate catch-up vaccination or data inconsistency.
